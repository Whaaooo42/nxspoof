Guide 🎮

Your Mac makes a Wi-Fi network. Your Switch joins it and believes it's online, but it can't reach Nintendo or the internet. Your Mac can still talk to the Switch (FTP, for example).

While NXSPOOF runs, your Mac is offline: its Wi-Fi is busy being the hotspot. It comes back when you stop. (Unless your Mac is also on Ethernet: then it stays online, and the consoles still get nothing.)

Start

  1. Double-click NXSPOOF.app. The dashboard opens in your browser (no password yet). The very first time, macOS asks whether NXSPOOF may use the folder it's in: click Allow.
  2. On a new Mac, open ⚙️ Configure first and press 🛠️ Fix selected: one password in macOS's window, and it gets the Mac ready and tells you what you need to do yourself (Setup).
  3. Press ▶ Start NXSPOOF. macOS asks for your Mac password in its own window. No Terminal window: NXSPOOF runs in the background. (If the Mac isn't ready, the page says why first, and offers 🛠️ Fix and start: one password for both.)
  4. Wait a few seconds for READY. The page now shows the run; the 📡 chip shows your hotspot's name.
  5. Turn on your Switch. It joins the hotspot by itself (or pick it in the Switch's System Settings → Internet).

☕ While NXSPOOF runs, your Mac stays awake, like during a film: the screen can still turn off. Closing the lid still puts it to sleep, and that ends the run: when the Mac wakes, NXSPOOF stops cleanly (😴), resumes nothing, and your Mac stays on your home Wi-Fi. ▶ Start again when you need it.

Stop

Click ■ Stop on the dashboard (or menu 2 in menu.command). Firewall off, a summary of each Switch's visit, Internet Sharing off, the virtual cable out: your Mac gets its internet back.

Only a Stop stops NXSPOOF (or your Mac going to sleep: see below). Closing the dashboard's page while it runs: a small macOS dialog asks Stop NXSPOOF or Keep running (the default: your Switch stays connected). Open NXSPOOF.app again whenever you want: the run is there at once, its whole log from the start, and its ■ Stop button.

The dashboard

Your browser shows http://localhost:8800 (the same address each time). Only your Mac can see it.

A card for each Switch

Online or away, how long it's been there, whether it passed its online check, its FTP server, what it tried to reach. ✏️ Edit (name, color, FTP port, notes…) and 🗑️ Remove on each known Switch.

The live log

What's happening, in plain words. Technical for every detail; Oldest first / Newest first for the order. Its picker (● Live ▾) shows any past run, with its checklist.

⚙️ Configure

Left: is this Mac ready, and 🛠️ Fix selected. Right: every setting, with what it does. Changes wait for Save; some apply at once, the others at the next start (each says which). 📤 Export / 📥 Import your settings.

The buttons

📂 Open in FileZilla when a Switch's FTP server is up. ➕ Add to settings.json on a Switch NXSPOOF doesn't know yet. ➕ Add a console by its MAC address, before it even connects. 📖 Help: these pages.

The hotspot's name, channel and password are macOS's: This Mac's hotspot line opens System Settings for them. NXSPOOF only reads them.

A friend's Switch

Let it join the hotspot. Its card shows a small form: type a name (and model, owner, color if you like), then ➕ Add to settings.json. From then on, NXSPOOF knows it by name.

The menu (Terminal)

Prefer the Terminal? Double-click menu.command: everything, by number. 1 start, 2 stop, 3/4 watch it live (technical or casual), 5 the dashboard; then 6 Past runs, 7 This Mac, 8 Development, 9 Docs, numbered from 1 again inside. b back, q quit. What doesn't fit the moment is grayed out (but still answers). All of it, screen by screen: The two doors.

Where things are

Every run is saved with all its technical details in NXSPOOF's data folder: open the data shortcut in the nx-spoof folder, then logs/ (last_run.log is always the latest). Your settings live there too, so you can move or rename the nx-spoof folder freely: nothing is lost.

If a file called MEMO.md appears in the folder, the code watchdog has a note about code that's getting complicated. Nothing is broken; just mention it to Claude.

What happensTry this

NXSPOOF.app won't open ("unidentified developer")Right-click it, Open, then Open again. Only once (the same for menu.command)
▶ Start: nothing happens after the passwordThe dashboard's log says why. Or start from the menu (1): the Terminal prints the same answer
Your Mac still has no internet after stoppingStop again, or turn Internet Sharing off in System Settings → General → Sharing

Anything else: Troubleshooting (with a search box), and 🐞 Report when it doesn't help.
